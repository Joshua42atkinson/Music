# **Preface: The Arithmetic of the Soul**

Bertrand's teaching method embodies Leibniz's observation that "Music is a hidden arithmetic exercise of the soul" by rooting musical expression in a master curriculum of physical coordinates and numeric relationships. This somatic blueprint transitions from the raw geometric matrix of the strings to a system where scale degrees and chord tones are visualized as "Numbers\!"—the critical node in the learning flowchart.  
By treating the C Major scale as a "seed" and mapping its exact string-by-string layout through fretboard coordinates, students engage in a physical coordinate lookup map. This approach transforms abstract theory into a tangible arithmetic of the fretboard, where foundational half-step rules and interval games like the "1, 4, 44" sequence create a somatic bridge between the hands, the ears, and the mathematical symmetry of the instrument.

# **Module 1: The String-to-String Coordinate Matrix**

## **String Geometry & Names**

In our standard configuration, we define the strings by their physical weight and position. String \#6 is the fattest (low E), leading sequentially to A (\#5), D (\#4), G (\#3), B (\#2), and finally the high e (\#1). To remember strings 1 through 6 in reverse order, use the mnemonic: "Every Boy Gets Desert After Eight."

## **The 5th Fret Matching Rule (The Standard Pattern)**

The guitar is a beautifully symmetric instrument governed by the 5th fret rule. This pattern allows you to find identical frequencies across different strings:

* E to A: Play open E (String 6\) and match it with the note on Fret 5\. This A is identical in frequency to the open 5th string.  
* A to D: Play open A (String 5). Fret 5 on this string is D, matching the open String 4\.  
* D to G: Play open D (String 4). Fret 5 is G, matching the open String 3\.

## **The Standard Tuning "Pothole" (The Break in the Pattern)**

Navigating the fretboard requires understanding the physical break in our symmetry. When you play open G (String 3), you must drop to Fret 4 (not 5\) to find B, which matches the open String 2\. This is a crucial "half-step shift" that moves all geometric scale coordinates up by one fret whenever you cross onto strings 1 and 2\.

## **Returning to Pattern**

After the pothole, the standard symmetry returns: Play open B (String 2), and Fret 5 is e, matching the open String 1\.

# **Module 2: The "If E is 1, What is 4?" Interval Game**

Bertrand uses a conversational "applied theory" game to build immediate ear-to-fretboard coordination. By assigning the number 1 to a root note and finding its perfect fourth (4), we unlock the vocabulary of countless songs. We practice the 1, 4, 44 sequence (often recognized as the Bridal March or Christmas tree example):

* If E is 1: 4 is A (E-A). Play 1, 4, 44\.  
* If A is 1: 4 is D (A-D). Play 1, 4, 44\.  
* If D is 1: 4 is G (D-G). Play 1, 4, 44\.  
* If G is 1: 4 is C (G-C). Play 1, 4, 44\.  
* If C is 1: 4 is F (C-F). Play 1, 4, 44\.  
* If F is 1: 4 is Bb (F-Bb). Play 1, 4, 44\.

This physical coordinate lookup map helps visualize these intervals within the first 5 frets, creating a somatic bridge between your hands and your ears.

# **Module 3: Mapping the C Major Scale (The 5-Fret Seed)**

The C Major scale—containing no sharps or flats—is the "seed" of Western music. Within the first 5 frets, we can map its exact string-by-string layout:

* Fret 1: F (Str 6), C (Str 2), F (Str 1).  
* Fret 2: B (Str 5), E (Str 4), A (Str 3).  
* Fret 3: G (Str 6), C (Str 5), F (Str 4), D (Str 2), G (Str 1).  
* Fret 4: B (Str 3).  
* Fret 5: A (Str 6), D (Str 5), G (Str 4), E (Str 2), A (Str 1).

## **The Geometric Key of Western Harmony**

When we look at the C scale in scale degree numbers (Roots as 1, Seconds as 2, etc.), we discover a foundational half-step physical spacing rule. Half steps always occur between notes 3 and 4 (E & F) and 7 and 8 (B & C). On the fretboard, these notes are physically adjacent—sitting right next to each other with zero empty frets between them. This spatial adjacency is the essential geometric key that defines the entire Western harmonic system.

# **V. Bertrand's Whiteboard Pedagogical Tree (The Somatic Learning Flowchart)**

Drawing from Bertrand’s whiteboard artifact, we define a master curriculum rooted in the physical reality of the instrument. This pedagogical tree represents the logical flow from raw coordinates to integrated musical expression.  
**1\. The Core Whiteboard Flowchart**  
\[Open 5-Fret String-to-String MAP\] \-\> \[C Scale Seed\] \-\> \[String-to-String Matching\] \-\> \[See Patterns & Intervals\] \-\> \[Numbers\! Chord & Scale Tones\]  
From the critical "Numbers\!" node, the somatic system branches into three synchronous pathways:

* **Path A (Harmonic):** \[Diatonic Chords\] \-\> \[Songs\] & \[Rhythm \-\> Fingerstyle\].  
* **Path B (Acoustic Feedback):** \[Ear/Hearing Training\] \-\> \[Voice Training\] (Sing what you play, play what you sing).  
* **Path C (Melodic/Vertical):** \[Vertiscales Everywhere (from any scale degree number)\] \-\> \[CAGED Numbers & Triads\] \-\> \[E Vertiscales & E Triad\].

**2\. The Four-Stage Maturation Sequence**  
The following curriculum milestones represent the hierarchical progression of the somatic guitarist:

* **Stage 1: Open Position MAP.** Prioritizing physical coordinates and string geometry over abstract theory.  
* **Stage 2: Scale-to-Triad Conversions.** Identifying the supportive harmonic beams located within the scale seed.  
* **Stage 3: Chords to Melody.** Developing asymmetrical interaction and dual-coding through vertical visualization.  
* **Stage 4: Songs.** Full reintegration of all elements into memory-making and musical fluency.
