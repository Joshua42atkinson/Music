# THE RESONANT MIRROR — Walking Mode
*Headphones On. Screen Off. Eyes Open. Ears Awake.*

An extension of the Resonant Mirror that works **without the guitar**, for when the student is walking, commuting, or simply moving through the world.

---

## The Core Insight

Bertrand's pedagogy has three stages: **BE** (imagine it), **DO** (play it), **PLAY** (express it).

The Resonant Mirror (guitar mode) covers DO and PLAY. But BE — the imagination, the inner ear, the silence before the sound — has no dedicated tool yet.

The Walking Mode IS the BE engine.

It trains the part of music that happens before you touch the instrument: the ability to *hear* a Perfect Fifth inside your own skull, without any external reference. Bertrand calls this the "Inner Ear." Every great musician has it. No app currently trains it.

---

## The Structure: 4 On / 4 Off

Borrowed from the Japanese interval walking practice and adapted for ear training:

### The "4 Minutes ON" (Sound)
The headphones are active. The student hears:
1. A **drone note** (their root, established in a previous Resonant Mirror session or chosen fresh).
2. A **narrative prompt** from the Troubadour (Bernard de Ventadorn, or Bertrand's recorded voice):
   > *"You are crossing the bridge at Poitiers. The river below hums a low A. Eleanor's messenger approaches from the far bank. He carries a chord you have never heard. Listen..."*
3. An **interval is revealed** — the system plays the target interval (e.g., Perfect Fifth) over the drone. The student hears what 3:2 *sounds* like, layered into the story.
4. A **call-and-response** — the system plays the interval, pauses, and the student must **hum or sing it back** through the headphone mic. The pitch detector confirms.

### The "4 Minutes OFF" (Silence)
The headphones go quiet. No sound at all. The student walks in actual silence.

During this phase, the student's job is to **hold the interval in their mind**. Can they still hear the Perfect Fifth ringing inside their skull, with no external reference? Can they feel the 3:2 ratio in their body's rhythm of walking?

This is Bertrand's "Imagine in the Silence" made literal.

When the 4 minutes end, the headphones come back on with a gentle chime, and the system plays the interval again. Did the student's inner ear drift? Or did they hold it?

### The Cycle
```
4 min ON  — Hear the interval. Sing it back. Story advances.
4 min OFF — Hold it in silence. Walk. Feel the ratio in your steps.
4 min ON  — Check: play the interval again. Did you hold it?
4 min OFF — Deeper silence. Try to hear TWO intervals now.
...repeat for 20-40 minutes (a walk)
```

## The Story Layer (Day_Dream Integration)

The 4-on periods are not just ear training drills. They are **chapters of a walking story**.

The Troubadour adventure data we already built (`troubadour.js`) has scenes, settings, mentor lines, and branching choices. In Walking Mode, these play out as an **audio drama**:

- **Scene narration** plays as ambient storytelling (text-to-speech or Bertrand's recorded voice)
- **Choices are made by singing**, not clicking. When the story says "Speak your name or Sing your name," the student literally hums or sings through the headphone mic.
- **The story branches based on pitch accuracy.** If you nail the interval during the challenge, the story takes the "bonus" path (e.g., `bonusLeadsTo: 'great-hall-recognized'`). If you miss, you get the standard path. The narrative literally responds to your ear training performance.

This makes the walk feel like you are *inside* a LitRPG audiobook where your voice is the controller.

## The Session Types

### Level 1: The Root Walk (Fret 1)
- Only one note: the Root (Unison)
- ON: Hear the drone. Sing it back. Hold it.
- OFF: Walk in silence. Can you still hear it?
- Story: Arrival at Poitiers. The guard asks your name.

### Level 2: The Tension Walk (Frets 2-3)
- Two intervals: Root + Minor 2nd, Root + Major 2nd
- ON: Hear the dissonance. Feel the tension. Sing the step.
- OFF: Hold the tension in silence. Feel it resolve in your body.
- Story: Meeting Peire d'Alvernha. The rival sneers.

### Level 3: The Harmony Walk (Frets 4-5)
- Two intervals: Minor 3rd, Major 3rd
- ON: Hear the color change (melancholy → joy). Sing both.
- OFF: Hold the emotional shift in silence. Which one are you?
- Story: Eleanor asks her question. Your answer determines the color.

### Level 4: The Architecture Walk (Frets 6-8)
- Three intervals: Perfect 4th, Tritone, Perfect 5th
- ON: The pillars and the demon. Hear the structure of Western music.
- OFF: Hold the 4th and 5th simultaneously in your mind. The tritone lurks between them.
- Story: Bernard's lesson. The ordeal. The reward.

### Level 5: The Full Choir Walk (Frets 9-12)
- All remaining intervals stacking
- ON: Full chromatic ear training. The system plays random intervals; you identify and sing them.
- OFF: Walk in silence carrying the entire chromatic scale in your inner ear.
- Story: The final performance. Eleanor stands. The octave returns.

## The Connection Back to the Guitar

When the student finishes their walk and picks up their guitar for a Resonant Mirror session, they will find that the intervals come faster. Their fingers will find the frets more quickly because their inner ear already *knows* where the notes are.

The Walking Mode trains the map. The Guitar Mode walks the map.

Together, they are the full BE → DO → PLAY cycle:
- **BE:** Walking Mode (imagine the interval in silence)
- **DO:** Resonant Mirror (find the interval on the fretboard)
- **PLAY:** Song Workshop (compose with the intervals you've mastered)
