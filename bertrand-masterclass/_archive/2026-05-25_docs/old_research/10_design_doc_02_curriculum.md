## III. THE CURRICULUM — The Chromatic Monomyth

### The Core Structural Insight

The Western chromatic scale has 12 tones. The Hero's Journey has 12 stages. This is not a coincidence exploited for marketing — it is a genuine pedagogical alignment: **the student's inner journey through fear, breakthrough, and mastery maps precisely onto the musical journey from a single root note to full harmonic freedom.**

The curriculum does not teach music theory in sequence and then apply it. It *is* a journey. Each chapter is a stage of initiation. The student does not advance because they passed a test — they advance because they have **heard something they could not un-hear.**

### Macro Sequence: Notes → Chords → Songs

The 12 chapters move through three irreversible thresholds:

```
CHAPTERS 1–4: NOTES
  The student learns what a single note means.
  They locate it, sing it, feel its resonance in their body.
  They discover tension and release — the fundamental grammar of music.

CHAPTERS 5–8: CHORDS
  Notes in relationship. The student learns that harmony is
  not theory — it is an emotional state that the fingers navigate.
  The CAGED system becomes the map.

CHAPTERS 9–12: SONGS
  Full fretboard fluency. The student improvises.
  No rules. No map visible on screen. Just the instrument and the voice inside.
```

### The Digital Binder — Self-Discovery Tool Map

The Digital Binder is not a separate app within the app. It is **the student's practice nook made digital** — always present, always safe, never demanding.

The 12 tools follow a psychological arc from **safety → mastery → freedom:**

```
SAFETY & COMMITMENT (Frets 1–2)
  Fret 1:  Breathing Gate       — "Am I safe here?"           (©SHEARL)
  Fret 2:  Practice Timer       — "Can I commit to this?"     (©SHEARL)

PERCEPTION & LISTENING (Frets 3–4)
  Fret 3:  Pitch Room           — "Can I hear myself?"        (©PLING!)
  Fret 4:  Metronome            — "Can I surrender to time?"  (©SHEARL)

SPATIAL AWARENESS (Frets 5–6)
  Fret 5:  Interval Visualizer  — "How do notes relate?"      (©SHEARL)
  Fret 6:  Fretboard Explorer   — "Can I face the whole neck?"(©SHEARL)

INTEGRATION & PRECISION (Frets 7–8)
  Fret 7:  PLING! Trainer       — "Can I sing and play?"      (©PLING!)
  Fret 8:  Microtonal Tracker   — "How precise am I really?"  (©FHEAL)

MASTERY & FREEDOM (Frets 9–12)
  Fret 9:  Vertiscale Engine    — ⭐ THE GAME                 (©SHEARL → ©PLING! → ©FHEAL)
  Fret 10: Async Assessor       — "Can I be seen?"            (©FHEAL)
  Fret 11: Multi-Key Hub        — "Can I see the whole?"      (©FHEAL)
  Fret 12: Rhythm Engine        — "Can I play free?"          (©FHEAL)
```

### Key Design Principle: Tools Are Called, Not Chosen

In Chapters 1–8, the SlideViewer should *suggest* (and eventually require) specific tools before advancing. A student in Chapter 7 should not be able to skip the PLING! Trainer exercise without at least opening it. The curriculum data flow dictates which tool is active — the student does not browse a catalog, they receive an invitation.
