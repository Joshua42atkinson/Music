## Sample 2: Stuck on the Tritone (Fret 7 — The Ordeal)

**Context:** Student has been practicing F to B and finds it ugly.

**Student:** "The tritone sounds wrong. I don't like it."

**Troubadour:**
> The tritone is supposed to sound unsettled — that is its power. Sing F to B slowly. Feel the tension in your throat. Do not resolve it yet. The discomfort IS the lesson. Music is not only beauty. It is also crisis, breakthrough, and the courage to sit in the unresolved. Over.

## How to Evaluate These

**Give each sample a score 1-5 on:**

1. **Sounds like Bertrand** — Would Bertrand actually say this?
2. **Pedagogically sound** — Would this actually help a student learn?
3. **Tone** — Gentle, not judgmental? Encouraging, not demanding?
4. **Actionable** — Does it give a concrete next step?
5. **Language** — Natural English/French? Not robotic?

**If the average score is < 3.5:** The system prompt needs work.
**If the average score is ≥ 4:** We're close. Minor prompt tweaks needed.
**If the average score is ≥ 4.5:** Ship it.

## To Get ACTUAL Audio (Not Text)

**Option A: Browser TTS (Now, Generic Voice)**
- Use browser's built-in speech synthesis
- Pros: Works immediately, no setup
- Cons: Sounds like a robot, not Bertrand

**Option B: OuteTTS via llama.cpp (Local, Generic Voice)**
- Requires downloading OuteTTS model (~500MB) + WavTokenizer (~1GB)
- Pros: Local, fast, decent quality
- Cons: Still generic, not Bertrand's voice

**Option C: Voice Clone (Requires Bertrand's Recording)**
- Bertrand records 30 seconds of speech
- Train a voice model (Piper, Coqui, RVC)
- Pros: Actually sounds like Bertrand
- Cons: Needs Bertrand's time and a clean recording

**Recommendation:** Start with Option A (browser TTS) for beta. Move to Option C when Bertrand records his voice.
